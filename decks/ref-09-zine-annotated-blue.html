02northbeam — project proposal

about us

the studio, kreuzberg — 2044

northbeam is a studio of eleven people working on brand systems for companies that ship software. we were founded in 2038 by two designers who were tired of handing over files that nobody could maintain, and we have kept the same rule since: whatever we draw, someone else has to be able to run.

that rule decides everything else. it is why our deliverable is never a folder of images, why we write down the reasoning next to the artwork, and why we measure what we make instead of arguing about it.

we work in short blocks with the people who will own the result. no long silences, no reveal at the end. you see the work while it is still cheap to change, and we spend the last week of every engagement teaching rather than drawing.

every engagement ends with a half-day where we hand over the reasoning, not only the files: what each decision was for, what we would change first, and which parts are safe to break.

the studio is deliberately small. eleven people is the largest number that can sit around one table and still hear each other, and everything about the way we work assumes that table exists.

we take four projects a year. it is the number that lets the same two people stay on a project from the first conversation to the last, which is the only way we know to keep a promise made in week one.

the problem

the identity works when you draw it and fails when anyone else does. four teams ship four versions of the same button, the palette drifts one hue per quarter, and nobody can say which file is current. none of that is a drawing problem: every one of those decisions was made by someone who could not find the answer in under a minute.

a system nobody can run is a system that only exists on the day it is presented.
